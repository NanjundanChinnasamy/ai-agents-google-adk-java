# Finance Advisor Project Grounding Knowledge: Market Research Framework

## Title
Structured Market Research & Information Grounding Framework

## Purpose
Establishes the methodology for conducting real-time equity investigations, organizing external evidence from Google Search, and separating verifiable factual disclosures from market speculation or subjective commentary.

## Definitions
- **Material Non-Public Information (MNPI)**: Confidential corporate data that would influence an investor's decision; research must rely strictly on publicly disseminated records.
- **Corporate Filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, Annual Reports)**: Legally mandated, auditable disclosures submitted to securities regulators detailing official operational and financial results.
- **Consensus Estimates**: The mathematical average or median of forward projections (revenue, EPS) compiled by sell-side equity analysts.
- **Earnings Surprise**: The percentage differential between actual reported quarterly metrics and consensus expectations prior to the release.
- **Guidance Revisions**: Updates provided by corporate management altering forward projected financial performance.
- **Attributed Commentary**: Viewpoints, ratings, or price targets that can be explicitly traced back to an identifiable analyst, institution, or official speaker.

## Practical Explanation
Effective market research requires synthesizing real-time information through an orderly hierarchy:
1. **Primary Grounding (Official Disclosures)**: Regulatory filings, audited financial statements, company press releases, and transcripts of official earnings calls.
2. **Secondary Evidence (Attributed Coverage)**: Reputable financial news reporting, accredited industry publications, and named analyst notes.
3. **Tertiary Data (Market Sentiment)**: Broad commentary, social sentiment, and price momentum.

When current market news occurs, research must answer three questions:
- *What factual event occurred?* (Event identification)
- *What is the verifiable data?* (Exact numbers, dates, statements)
- *What are the analytical implications?* (Impact on revenue, margins, valuation, or competitive position)

## Examples
- **Earnings Investigation**: Company C reports Q2 earnings. Research gathers:
  - Factual Top/Bottom line: Revenue $12.4B (+9% YoY, beating consensus by 2%); EPS $1.15 (meeting consensus).
  - Management Guidance: Raised full-year revenue outlook by 1.5%.
  - Market Reaction: Share price traded up 3.4% post-announcement.
  - Analyst Consensus: Two major investment banks increased price targets citing margin resilience.

## Limitations
- News headlines often assert causal links ("Stock drops because of X") that cannot be econometrically verified.
- Short-term market price fluctuations frequently reflect transient liquidity or technical positioning rather than structural fundamental change.

## When This Knowledge Is Useful
- Guiding the investigation of recent news, earnings releases, corporate announcements, and regulatory actions.
- Structuring multi-source Google Search queries for current company information.
- Distinguishing verified empirical facts from subjective analytical interpretation.
