# Finance Advisor Project Grounding Knowledge: Master Glossary

## Title
Essential Investment & Financial Terminology Glossary

## Purpose
Provides authoritative, standardized definitions of foundational financial metrics and equity instruments used across the Finance Advisor project. It anchors agent responses in established corporate finance standards without speculation.

## Definitions
- **Common Stock (Share)**: A security representing fractional ownership in a corporation, giving holders residual claims on assets and net earnings, typically accompanied by voting rights.
- **Market Capitalization**: The aggregate market valuation of a publicly traded company's outstanding equity ($Price \times Shares$).
- **Revenue**: Gross inflows generated from core operating activities before deducting costs, expenses, or taxes.
- **Net Income**: The bottom-line profit remaining after all operating expenses, cost of goods sold, financing interest, taxes, and depreciation have been accounted for.
- **Earnings Per Share (EPS)**: Net income available to common shareholders divided by the weighted average number of common shares outstanding during the period.
- **Price-to-Earnings Ratio (P/E)**: The market price per share divided by annual earnings per share, reflecting the price paid per unit of current or forward earnings.
- **Price-to-Book Ratio (P/B)**: The ratio comparing a company's market price to its accounting net asset book value.
- **Dividend & Dividend Yield**: A dividend is a corporate cash or stock distribution of profits. Dividend yield is the annualized payout divided by the current share price.
- **Free Cash Flow (FCF)**: Operating cash flow minus capital expenditures, indicating discretionary cash generation available for growth or capital returns.
- **Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)**: The annualized smoothed rate of geometric return over a multi-year period assuming reinvestment.

## Practical Explanation
These fundamental definitions establish common financial literacy. They allow an analyst or investor to describe a business quantitatively rather than relying on qualitative impressions. For example, comparing market capitalization across peers helps classify companies into relevant peer groups, while free cash flow highlights cash generation capability independent of non-cash accounting accruals.

## Examples
- A company with 100 million shares trading at $50 has a **Market Capitalization** of $5.0 billion (Mid/Large-Cap threshold).
- If that company earns $200 million in annual net income, its **EPS** is $2.00 ($\frac{\$200\text{M}}{100\text{M}}$).
- Its **Trailing P/E** is 25.0x ($\frac{\$50}{\$2.00}$).
- If it distributes $1.00 per share annually in dividends, its **Dividend Yield** is 2.0% ($\frac{\$1.00}{\$50} \times 100$), and its payout ratio is 50%.

## Limitations
- Accounting conventions (IFRS vs US GAAP) can alter how revenue is recognized and how expenses/depreciation are scheduled.
- Static definitions do not reflect future business model transitions or competitive disruptions.
- Market capitalization reflects current trading price, which fluctuates with broader liquidity and sentiment.

## When This Knowledge Is Useful
- Responding to foundational questions about what financial terms mean (e.g., "What is a stock?", "Explain EPS", "What is market cap?").
- Defining terms before conducting deeper corporate valuation or portfolio discussions.
- Explaining the mechanics behind common equity ratios.
