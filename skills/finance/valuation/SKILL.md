# Valuation Skill

## Purpose & Scope
This skill guides the objective analysis of equity valuation. It establishes that valuation multiples cannot be classified as strictly "cheap" or "expensive" without rigorous context, including future growth expectations, industry benchmarks, cost of capital, and cyclical positioning.

## Core Valuation Multiples & Methods

### 1. Price-to-Earnings (Trailing & Forward P/E)
- **Trailing P/E**: $\frac{\text{Current Price}}{\text{Past 12 Months EPS}}$. Reflects historically realized earnings; can be distorted by one-time gains/charges or cyclical peaks.
- **Forward P/E**: $\frac{\text{Current Price}}{\text{Consensus Next 12 Months Estimated EPS}}$. Reflects forward analyst consensus; subject to forecast inaccuracies or revision risk.
- **Context Invariant**: A high P/E does not automatically mean a stock is overvalued; investors may be pricing in significant future earnings growth or high returns on capital. Conversely, a low P/E does not automatically indicate a bargain; it may reflect structural industry decline, deteriorating margins, or elevated business risk ("value trap").

### 2. Enterprise Value to EBITDA (EV/EBITDA)
- $\text{Enterprise Value (EV)} = \text{Market Cap} + \text{Total Debt} - \text{Cash and Equivalents}$.
- Neutralizes differences in capital structure (debt vs equity financing) and accounting depreciation policies. Commonly used for capital-intensive sectors and cross-border comparisons.

### 3. Price-to-Book (P/B)
- $\frac{\text{Market Price}}{\text{Book Value Per Share}}$.
- Relevant primarily for capital-heavy, financial, or asset-rich industries (banks, insurance, utilities, real estate). Less meaningful for asset-light software or intellectual property-driven enterprises.

### 4. Dividend Yield & Payout Ratio
- Dividend yield must be evaluated alongside the **Payout Ratio** ($\frac{\text{Dividends Paid}}{\text{Net Income}}$). Unusually elevated yields often signal market skepticism regarding the sustainability of future dividend distributions.

### 5. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Concept
- Premise: The intrinsic value of an asset equals the present value of its expected future cash flows, discounted at a rate reflecting the time value of money and operational risk (WACC).
- Multiples are shorthand heuristics; DCF models require explicit assumptions regarding long-term growth, terminal growth rates, and discount rates.

### 6. Relative Valuation (Sector & Historical Context)
- Multiples must be assessed against:
  1. *Historical 5-year average multiple* for the same ticker.
  2. *Direct peer / industry cohort multiples*.
  3. *PEG Ratio (P/E to Growth)*: $\frac{\text{P/E Ratio}}{\text{Annual EPS Growth Rate}}$ to benchmark valuation against expansion speed.

## Communication Principles
- Never pronounce: "Stock X is cheap" or "Stock Y is overvalued."
- Always state: "Stock X trades at 28x P/E, which reflects an expectation of robust earnings expansion relative to its sector median of 18x. Key considerations include whether sustained growth justifies this multiple."
