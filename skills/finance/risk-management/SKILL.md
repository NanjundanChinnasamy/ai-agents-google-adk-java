# Risk Management Skill

## Purpose & Scope
This skill provides a systematic taxonomy for evaluating the dimensions of financial risk in investment holdings and portfolios. Risk management focuses on downside exposure, capital preservation, and identifying single-point vulnerabilities.

## The Taxonomy of Investment Risks

### 1. Systematic vs Unsystematic Risk
- **Systematic (Market) Risk**: Macroeconomic shocks, broad interest rate adjustments, inflation cycles, and geopolitical crises that affect all market participants simultaneously. Cannot be eliminated through pure equity diversification.
- **Unsystematic (Company-Specific) Risk**: Operational failures, executive turnover, litigation, supply chain disruptions, or localized product recalls unique to a single issuer. Can be largely diversified away across uncorrelated holdings.

### 2. Concentration Risks
- **Single-Stock Concentration**: Significant allocation (> 10-25%) in a single security, exposing overall capital to issuer-specific solvency or execution failure.
- **Sector Concentration**: Excessive weighting in a single sector (e.g. 50% in Technology or Energy), making the portfolio hypersensitive to industry regulatory shifts or commodity cycles.
- **Geographic & Sovereign Concentration**: Overexposure to a single national economy or regulatory jurisdiction, heightening vulnerability to localized political, tax, or legal changes.

### 3. Structural & Market Dynamic Risks
- **Liquidity Risk**: The inability to enter or exit an asset position swiftly at fair market price without substantial price slippage. Common in small-cap equities, private placements, or illiquid credit.
- **Currency (FX) Risk**: Valuation volatility resulting from fluctuations in exchange rates between the asset's trading currency and the investor's home base currency.
- **Valuation Risk**: Acquiring sound companies at historically stretched multiples, leaving no margin of safety if future growth decelerates even modestly.
- **Regulatory & Legal Risk**: Potential disruption to revenue or business model arising from new legislation, antitrust scrutiny, import/export tariffs, or price caps.

### 4. Volatility & Drawdown Metrics
- **Volatility ($\sigma$ / Standard Deviation)**: The statistical dispersion of returns over a specific timeframe. High volatility indicates greater price unpredictability.
- **Maximum Drawdown**: The peak-to-trough decline in asset or portfolio valuation before a new peak is reached. Measures the magnitude of acute capital loss experienced during adverse periods.
- **Beta ($\beta$)**: Measure of an individual asset's historical sensitivity to broader market movements. A beta > 1.0 implies greater amplitude than the benchmark index.

## Analytical Standards
- Highlight risk sources clearly by type.
- Present risk mitigations conceptually (e.g. asset rebalancing, multi-sector diversification, time horizon matching) without issuing prescriptive trade instructions.
