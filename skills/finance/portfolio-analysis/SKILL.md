# Portfolio Analysis Skill

## Purpose & Scope
This skill guides the structural and qualitative evaluation of an investment portfolio. It teaches how to conceptualize asset distribution, risk contribution, and balance without performing arithmetic calculations (which are deferred to specialized tools).

## Core Architectural Concepts

### 1. Asset Allocation & Capital Distribution
- Asset allocation is the foundational division of total wealth across broad asset classes (Equities, Fixed Income, Real Assets, Cash Equivalents).
- Historical studies demonstrate that top-level asset allocation is the primary driver of long-term return variability and risk profile.

### 2. Diversification & Correlation
- **The Diversification Principle**: Combining assets that respond differently to identical macroeconomic conditions reduces overall portfolio volatility without necessarily diminishing expected return.
- **Correlation ($\rho$)**:
  - *Positive Correlation (+1.0)*: Assets move in tandem.
  - *Zero Correlation (0.0)*: Asset price movements are statistically independent.
  - *Negative Correlation (-1.0)*: Assets move in opposing directions, providing defensive hedging.
  - *Insight*: Diversification works best when holdings exhibit low or non-linear correlations during stress regimes.

### 3. Exposure Profiles
- **Sector Exposure**: Tracking the distribution across industry verticals (Technology, Healthcare, Financials, Consumer Staples, Energy, Industrials). Excessive clustering in one sector elevates vulnerability to sector-specific policy or cyclical downturns.
- **Geographic Footprint**: Assessing exposure across domestic markets, developed international economies, and emerging markets to balance regional growth opportunities against currency and geopolitical risks.

### 4. Position Sizing & Concentration Constraints
- **Core-Satellite Model**: Anchoring the portfolio in broad, liquid core holdings (e.g. index/large-cap anchors) while deploying smaller, controlled position sizing (e.g. 1-5% allocations) for high-conviction or tactical ideas.
- **Single-Position Thresholds**: Evaluating whether any individual stock exceeds institutional prudential guidelines (such as > 15-25% of total invested portfolio capital).

### 5. Investment Horizon & Risk Budgeting
- **Time Horizon**: Longer horizons (10+ years) provide capacity to absorb interim equity drawdown and volatility in exchange for compounding growth. Short horizons (< 3 years) require greater emphasis on capital preservation and liquidity.
- **Risk Contribution**: An asset's risk to the portfolio depends not only on its standalone volatility, but on its weight and covariance with the remaining holdings.

## Guidelines for Discussion
- Focus on conceptual balance, structural trade-offs, and diversification logic.
- Avoid performing manual arithmetic; address allocation percentages conceptually.
- Do not prescribe exact rebalancing buy/sell trades; highlight structural observations for decision support.
